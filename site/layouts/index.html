A small menu-bar app for the Mac. Your camera's HDMI feed goes to the call through a capture card; LensControl changes exposure, white balance, focus and mic over USB, so you never have to reach for the camera mid-call.

Get it for macOS View on GitHub

Free and open source. macOS 14 or later. Built and tested with the Nikon Z5 II.

How it works

Two cables, two jobs. The capture card carries the picture. USB carries the settings.

Exposure

Shutter from 1/30 to 1/8000, aperture, ISO from 100 to 204800, auto ISO with a maximum, and EV from −3 to +3 in ⅓ steps.

White balance

The camera's movie white-balance modes, and colour temperature from 2500 to 10000 K in 10 K steps.

Focus and mic

AF-S, AF-F or manual focus, a one-shot “Autofocus now”, and mic sensitivity: Auto, High or Medium.

Movie settings, not stills

The camera keeps separate photo and video settings. LensControl edits the video ones, which is what the HDMI feed shows.

Named presets

Save every setting as “Daylight desk” or “Evening lamp” and apply it with ⌘1 to ⌘9. If the camera refuses a value, LensControl tells you which and applies the rest.

Popover or window

A menu-bar popover for a five-second change, or a floating window with Keep on top to park beside the call. Both stay in sync.

Live preview

A 640×360 USB live view at up to 20 fps to judge exposure and colour, right in the panel.

Quiet

Talks to the camera through libgphoto2, in-process. No camera-brand software, reads and writes in about 2 ms, and the camera is released whenever the panel is closed.

Requirements

  • macOS 14 Sonoma or later, with libgphoto2 from Homebrew: brew install libgphoto2
  • Camera in video mode, USB set to PTP (the default), connected directly or through a hub
  • An HDMI capture card for the picture; USB only carries control
  • Built and tested with the Nikon Z5 II. Other cameras are untested.
